B Check my payment protection

Seven claims, each answered by something that actually ran — a real delivery fired at your endpoint, or a real query against Razorpay. Not a configuration check.

1 Inspect any integration

Point Raze at a real integration. It clones the repository, finds the code that receives Razorpay webhooks, and reports what is wrong with it — with the file, the line and the evidence. Nothing is executed at this step.

2 What happens without Raze

A merchant with those defects is started for real, with its own database. Five probes fire genuine captured Razorpay deliveries at it — the same retry ladder Razorpay actually sends — and the result is read back out of the merchant's own table. This is business state, not status codes.

3 What it costs

Money, derived from what just happened — not from a study. Every figure names where it came from. Orders nobody paid for are counted separately and never called revenue loss.

Run the probes first.

4 The same code, behind Raze

The merchant's handler is not modified. The same defective code runs behind the Raze runtime, and the identical probes are fired again.

5 Live Razorpay

Create a real Test Mode payment link, pay it, and watch the delivery arrive on the right. The order id shown here is the id in your Razorpay dashboard. Then sever delivery entirely: reconciliation recovers the payment by asking Razorpay what it recorded, and the ledger names what genuinely never came.
